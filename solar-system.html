HOW IT FORMED

Born from a cloud

About 4.6 billion years ago, a cloud of gas and dust started to fall together. Most of it became the Sun. The rest spun into a flat disc. In this disc, dust stuck together to make rocks, and rocks grew into planets. Near the Sun it was hot, so only rocky planets formed. Far away it was cold, so giant planets of gas and ice grew.

THE SUN

Our star

The Sun holds 99.86% of all the mass in the solar system. It is a star about 4.6 billion years old.

About 1.39 million km
Surface
About 5,500 °C
Core

Turns hydrogen into helium

Past

Ancient people worshipped the Sun. Galileo studied sunspots in the 1600s. In the 1900s scientists learned that the Sun shines by nuclear fusion.

Present

NASA's Parker Solar Probe has gone closer to the Sun than any craft before. ESA's Solar Orbiter and India's Aditya-L1 also watch it. The Sun has an 11-year cycle of activity.

Past

People have known Mercury since ancient times. The Mariner 10 probe visited it in 1974 and 1975. NASA's MESSENGER orbited it from 2011 to 2015 and mapped all of it. It also found ice in dark craters near the poles.

Present

ESA and JAXA's BepiColombo is due to start orbiting Mercury in late 2026.

Fun fact: One day on Mercury (sunrise to sunrise) lasts 176 Earth days. That is longer than its year.

Past

Soviet Venera probes landed on Venus in the 1970s and 1980s. They lasted only a short time in the heat. NASA's Magellan used radar to map almost the whole planet in the early 1990s.

Present

New missions are being planned, such as ESA's EnVision. India also plans a Venus orbiter. Japan's Akatsuki studied Venus's clouds until 2024.

Fun fact: Venus spins backwards, so the Sun rises in the west.

Past

Earth formed about 4.5 billion years ago. For a long time people thought it was the centre of everything. In 1543 Copernicus said the planets go around the Sun. The first photos of Earth from space came in the 1960s.

Present

Satellites watch our weather, oceans and climate every day. The International Space Station has had people on board all the time since November 2000.

Fun fact: Earth is not a perfect ball. It is slightly wider at the middle because it spins.

The Perseverance rover (landed 2021) is collecting rock samples. The small helicopter Ingenuity flew there in 2021. It was the first powered flight on another planet.

Fun fact: Olympus Mons is about 22 km high. That is nearly three times the height of Mount Everest.

Past

In 1610 Galileo saw four of Jupiter's moons through his telescope. This showed that not everything goes around Earth. Probes like Voyager and Galileo studied it later.

Present

NASA's Juno has orbited Jupiter since 2016. ESA's JUICE (launched 2023) and NASA's Europa Clipper (launched 2024) are on their way. They should arrive around 2030 and 2031.

Fun fact: The Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth. It has been seen for over 150 years.

Past

Galileo saw strange shapes beside Saturn in 1610. In 1655 Huygens worked out that they were rings. Voyager 1 and 2 passed by in 1980 and 1981. Cassini orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017, and its Huygens probe landed on the moon Titan in 2005.

Present

NASA plans to launch a flying robot called Dragonfly in 2028 to explore Titan.

Fun fact: Its moon Enceladus shoots out jets of water, and Titan has lakes of liquid methane.

William Herschel found Uranus in 1781. It was the first planet discovered with a telescope. Only one probe has ever visited it: Voyager 2, in 1986.

No mission is flying there yet. Scientists have named a Uranus orbiter as a top goal for the next ten years.

Fun fact: Uranus is tilted by about 98 degrees, so it rolls around the Sun like a ball.

Past

Neptune was found in 1846. Scientists used maths to work out where it must be, because Uranus was not moving as expected. Voyager 2 visited in 1989 and is still the only probe to do so.

Present

No probe is on the way. The James Webb telescope has taken new infrared pictures of its rings.

Fun fact: Winds on Neptune can reach about 2,000 km/h. That is the fastest in the solar system.

THE MOON

Earth's friend

The Moon is 3,474 km wide and 384,400 km away. The same side always faces Earth. It most likely formed when a Mars-sized rock hit Earth long ago.

Past

Luna 2 reached the Moon in 1959. Apollo 11 landed there in 1969. Twelve people walked on it between 1969 and 1972.

Present

India's Chandrayaan-3 landed near the south pole in 2023. China's Chang'e-6 brought back rocks from the far side in 2024. NASA's Artemis programme plans to send people back.

SMALL WORLDS

Dwarf planets, asteroids and comets

Pluto and dwarf planets

Pluto was called a planet from 1930 to 2006. New Horizons flew past it in 2015. Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake are dwarf planets too.

Asteroid belt

Millions of rocks orbit between Mars and Jupiter. In 2022 NASA's DART crashed into an asteroid to test how to push one away.

Comets

Balls of ice that grow bright tails near the Sun. Halley's Comet comes back about every 76 years. Next is 2061.

Kuiper belt and Oort cloud

The Kuiper belt is a ring of icy objects past Neptune. The Oort cloud is a huge shell of comets far beyond. Scientists have not seen it directly.
