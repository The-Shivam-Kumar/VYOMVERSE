CHAPTER 5

INDIA IN SPACE

From an old astronomer in 476 CE to Moon landings today. This is the story of how India reached for the sky.

Old roots1960s to 2000sBig missionsSpace for daily lifeRocketsPeopleFuture plans

OLD ROOTS

Indians have always watched the sky

Aryabhata (born 476 CE)

He wrote that Earth spins on its axis, which is why the stars seem to move. He also said eclipses are caused by shadows.

Brahmagupta (born 598 CE)

He wrote about the Sun, Moon and planets, and used zero in his calculations.

Jantar Mantar (1724 onwards)

Maharaja Jai Singh II built giant stone observatories in Delhi and Jaipur to track the Sun, Moon and stars.

Modern science

Meghnad Saha and Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar made key discoveries about stars in the 1900s.

1960s TO 2000s

Building from nothing

India started with very little money. It built its programme step by step, with the idea of using space to help ordinary people.

  1. 1962

    The space journey begins

    India sets up a space research committee. Dr Vikram Sarabhai leads it. He is called the father of the Indian space programme.

  2. 1963

    First rocket

    On 21 November a small rocket lifts off from Thumba in Kerala. Parts were carried by bicycle and bullock cart. A church was used as an office.

  3. 1969

    ISRO is born

    The Indian Space Research Organisation starts on 15 August. Its home is in Bengaluru.

  4. 1975

    Aryabhata

    India's first satellite, named after the great astronomer, goes up on 19 April. A Soviet rocket carried it.

  5. 1979

    Bhaskara-1

    India's first satellite for watching Earth.

  6. 1980

    Rohini on SLV-3

    India puts a satellite in orbit with its own rocket for the first time. Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam led the project.

  7. 1983

    INSAT system

    INSAT-1B starts a network of satellites for TV, phone links and weather.

  8. 1984

    Rakesh Sharma

    He flies to space on a Soviet craft and spends about 8 days on the Salyut 7 station.

  9. 1988

    IRS-1A

    India's remote sensing satellites start to map crops, water and forests.

  10. 1994

    PSLV works

    The PSLV rocket becomes India's trusted workhorse.

  11. 2001

    GSLV

    India launches its first heavier rocket. Later it builds its own cryogenic engine.

BIG MISSIONS

Recent achievements

  1. 2008

    Chandrayaan-1

    India's first Moon mission. It helped find signs of water on the Moon. A probe from it reached the Moon's surface.

  2. 2013 to 2014

    Mangalyaan

    The Mars Orbiter Mission launched in 2013 and reached Mars on 24 September 2014. India was the first country to reach Mars orbit on its first try. It cost about ₹450 crore, which is very low for a Mars mission. It worked for about 8 years.

  3. 2015

    AstroSat

    India's first space telescope for studying stars and galaxies in many kinds of light.

  4. 2017

    104 satellites

    On 15 February one PSLV rocket put 104 satellites into orbit. It was a world record at the time.

  5. 2019

    Chandrayaan-2

    The orbiter still works. The lander lost contact just before touching down on 7 September. ISRO used the lessons to try again.

  6. 2022

    First private rocket

    Skyroot's Vikram-S, from a start-up, becomes the first private rocket launched from India.

  7. 2023

    Chandrayaan-3

    On 23 August the Vikram lander touched down near the Moon's south pole. The rover Pragyan drove on the surface. India became the 4th country to land softly on the Moon. 23 August is now National Space Day.

  8. 2023

    Aditya-L1

    Launched 2 September. It reached its spot about 1.5 million km from Earth in January 2024 and studies the Sun.

  9. 2024

    XPoSat and Spadex

    XPoSat studies black holes and neutron stars. On 30 December, India launched Spadex to test joining two satellites in space.

  10. 2025

    Docking, NISAR and Shubhanshu Shukla

    Spadex satellites joined in space on 16 January. India became the 4th country to do this. NISAR, a radar satellite made with NASA, launched on 30 July. Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla flew to the ISS in June and spent about 18 days there.

FOR PEOPLE

How space helps daily life in India

Weather and storms

INSAT satellites help forecast cyclones. Early warnings save many lives.

TV and phones

Satellites carry TV channels, and help reach remote villages.

Farming

Satellite pictures show crops, water and soil, which helps farmers and planners.

Maps and navigation

India has its own navigation system called NavIC. It helps with maps, ships and trains.

Fishermen

Satellites find places with many fish and warn about rough seas.

Disaster help

Pictures from space show floods and fires, so rescue teams know where to go.

PEOPLE

Heroes of Indian space

Dr Vikram Sarabhai

Started the space programme in 1962.

Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Led the SLV-3 project, later became President of India.

Prof. Satish Dhawan

Led ISRO in its growing years. The Sriharikota launch centre is named after him.

Rakesh Sharma

The first Indian in space, in 1984.

Shubhanshu Shukla

The first Indian on the International Space Station, in 2025.

Women of ISRO

Women scientists such as Ritu Karidhal and M. Vanitha have led key missions.

Indian-origin astronauts

Kalpana Chawla and Sunita Williams flew with NASA.

FUTURE PLANS

What India plans next

These are plans. Dates can change, so check ISRO for the latest.

Gaganyaan

India's own crewed mission. It will send Indian astronauts to low Earth orbit and bring them home safely.

Bharatiya Antariksh Station

A planned Indian space station, targeted for around 2035.

Chandrayaan-4

A plan to bring Moon rocks back to Earth.

Venus orbiter

A planned mission to study Venus's thick air and surface.

Indian on the Moon

A goal to land an Indian on the Moon by 2040.

Private companies

Since 2020, IN-SPACe helps private firms like Skyroot and Agnikul to launch rockets.
