PAST TO PRESENT

The history of the universe

  1. 13.8 billion years ago

    The Big Bang

    The universe begins very hot and very small, and starts to grow. It was not an explosion in space. Space itself grew.

  2. A tiny fraction of a second

    Cosmic inflation (an idea)

    Many scientists think space grew extremely fast at the start. This could explain why the universe looks so smooth. It is not fully proven.

  3. 1 second to 3 minutes

    The first atoms' centres

    Tiny particles joined to make hydrogen, helium and a little lithium. This is why the universe is about three quarters hydrogen.

  4. About 380,000 years

    The first light

    The universe cooled, atoms formed, and light could finally travel. We still see this old light today. It is called the cosmic microwave background, found in 1965.

  5. Up to about 100 million years

    The dark ages

    There were no stars yet. Gravity slowly pulled gas into clumps.

  6. About 100 to 400 million years

    The first stars and galaxies

    The first stars switched on and the first galaxies grew. The James Webb telescope has seen galaxies from less than 300 million years after the Big Bang.

  7. About 10 billion years ago

    The busiest time for stars

    Stars were being born faster than at any other time.

  8. About 5 billion years ago

    Growth speeds up

    A mystery push called dark energy began to make the universe grow faster and faster.

  9. 4.6 billion years ago

    The Sun and planets form

    A cloud of gas and dust fell together. The Sun formed in the middle. The planets grew from the leftover material.

  10. 4.5 billion years ago

    Earth and the Moon

    Earth formed. A space rock about the size of Mars is thought to have hit it and made the Moon.

  11. At least 3.5 billion years ago

    Life on Earth

    The oldest clear signs of life are tiny microbes at least 3.5 billion years old.

  12. 66 million years ago

    An asteroid hits Earth

    A rock about 10 km wide hit Mexico. It helped end the age of the dinosaurs.

  13. About 300,000 years ago

    Modern humans

    Our species, Homo sapiens, appears in Africa.

  14. Today

    We read the story of the universe

    Telescopes like Hubble and James Webb let us look back across 13 billion years.

WHAT IT IS MADE OF

Mostly things we cannot see

Ordinary matter: stars, planets, gas and us5%
Dark matter: we only see its pull of gravity27%
Dark energy: makes the universe grow faster68%

Dark matter and dark energy are names for things scientists can measure, but do not yet understand.

THE PROOF

How do we know all this?

The universe is growing (1929)

Edwin Hubble found that far galaxies move away from us. The farther they are, the faster they go.

Old light (1965)

Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson found a faint glow from the early universe. Satellites later mapped it.

The first elements

The amounts of hydrogen and helium we see match what scientists calculate for the first minutes.

Mapping galaxies

Maps of millions of galaxies show where dark matter must be.

Ripples in space (2015)

LIGO detected gravitational waves from two black holes crashing together.

WHAT MAY HAPPEN

The future

These are scientists' predictions, not certain facts.

  1. In about 4.5 billion years

    Our galaxy meets Andromeda

    The Milky Way will join with the Andromeda galaxy. Stars are so far apart that very few will crash.

  2. In about 5 billion years

    The Sun grows huge

    The Sun will run low on fuel, swell up into a red giant, and then shrink into a small white dwarf.

  3. Trillions of years from now

    The stars go out

    New stars will stop forming. The last ones will fade. Most scientists expect a cold, dark universe.

OPEN QUESTIONS

What we do not know yet

What is dark matter?

We see its gravity but not the thing itself.

What is dark energy?

It might be a property of space, or something new.

How fast is the universe growing?

Two ways to measure it give slightly different answers.

Are we alone?

We know thousands of planets. We do not know if any has life.

What was before the Big Bang?

Nobody knows. It may not even be a meaningful question.
