LIFE CYCLE

How a star lives and dies

Almost every atom heavier than hydrogen and helium was made inside a star.

  1. Birth

    Cloud

    A cloud of gas and dust falls together because of gravity.

  2. Early life

    Baby star

    The middle gets hot as more material falls in.

  3. Long life

    Main sequence

    The star burns hydrogen. Our Sun will do this for about 10 billion years in total.

  4. Old age

    Red giant

    The fuel runs low. The star swells up and cools down.

  5. Small stars end

    White dwarf

    The Sun will end as a small, hot, Earth-sized star that slowly cools.

  6. Big stars end

    Supernova

    A star much bigger than the Sun explodes. It leaves a neutron star or a black hole. The blast spreads elements that make planets and people.

GALAXIES

Islands of stars

Milky Way

Our home galaxy. It is about 100,000 light-years wide and has 100 to 400 billion stars.

Andromeda

The nearest big galaxy, 2.5 million light-years away. It is moving towards us.

Three main shapes

Spiral (with arms), elliptical (smooth ball) and irregular (no clear shape).

Hubble's discovery

In the 1920s Hubble showed that other galaxies exist beyond ours.

NEBULAE

Clouds where stars are born

Orion Nebula

A star nursery about 1,300 light-years away. You can see it with your eyes as a fuzzy spot in Orion.

Crab Nebula

What is left of a star that exploded. People in several countries saw the blast in the year 1054.

Pillars of Creation

Tall towers of gas where new stars form. Hubble made them famous in 1995.

BLACK HOLES

Where gravity wins

A black hole pulls so hard that even light cannot escape. Some form when huge stars collapse. Giant ones, called supermassive, sit in the middle of most large galaxies. Ours is called Sagittarius A*. It is about 4 million times heavier than the Sun.

Past

Scientists first thought of the idea in the 1700s. Einstein's 1915 theory of gravity made it clearer.

Present

In 2015 LIGO heard two black holes crash together. In 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope took the first picture of one, in galaxy M87. In 2022 it showed ours.

Neutron stars

A neutron star is heavier than the Sun but only about 20 km wide. Some spin fast and flash like lighthouses. These are called pulsars. Jocelyn Bell Burnell found the first in 1967.

OTHER WORLDS

Planets around other stars

First find (1995)

Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz found a planet around a Sun-like star. They won the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Kepler and TESS

These NASA telescopes find planets by watching for tiny dips in a star's light.

Today

More than 6,000 planets are confirmed. The James Webb telescope studies the air around some of them.

The big question

Does any of them have life? We do not know yet.

Indian link: Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar worked out in 1930 that a dying star above about 1.4 times the Sun's mass cannot become a white dwarf. He won the 1983 Nobel Prize. Meghnad Saha explained in 1920 how to read a star's heat from its light.
